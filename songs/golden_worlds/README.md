The original bass lines, harmony, grooves and accompaniment figures are retained; the melodic parts use calmer sampled voices, smaller working ranges, repeated-note motifs and clearer breathing points. Each song remains 3–4 minutes.

## What changed

### Sideways Sun

A repeated vE4 pickup falls to C4, then returns with altered endings. Folk harp takes the quieter replies. The electric-piano comping, bass and broken beat retain their original notes.

### Pocket Algebra

A repeated third falls to the tonic. Low vibraphone and marimba trade the theme; the sixteen-bar solo develops it through pauses, short turns and a different second half. The bridge now briefly sounds its defining ^D.

### Parallax Arcade

A spacious mallet hook leaves room for the busy sequencer. The hook opens into the decatonic, crosses into ^D and returns with new replies. The former summit lead boost is removed.

### Two Windows

A low cello subject and a higher piano answer draw from complementary pentatonics. The duet has a written countervoice, the development changes mode, and the twelve-bar reunion brings back fragments without a high synthetic lead.

### Night Orchard

A two-note sigh moves between harp and low horn, with substantial rests. The golden-spectrum pad and glass figures continue around it. The bloom broadens the line gently, then the neighboring-key detour and return contract it.

## Verification and listening status

All five parse, render and remain within their declared local golden collections. The bass, harmony, timing and listed accompaniment events match the archived scores. There are no clipped samples, held melody/chord clashes or sustained sixth-step rubs of at least a quarter beat. Sampled parts stay within the published sample ranges. The five complete 1080p, 30 fps videos now use performance cards with large individual pitch names, chord-tone names, animated highlighting and 36-ET pitch strips. They reuse the verified melody-revision-2 audio.

The automated ear still reports shared-register warnings in some passages, a generic lead-range warning for the vibraphone’s valid F3, and some contrast/balance findings. These are recorded rather than “fixed” by restoring shrill octaves or a boosted lead. Short comping, rests, timbre and stereo placement separate the voices; the marimba in Parallax intentionally sits below the drums. See [verification.json](verification.json) for all findings and their disposition.

The revised versions await a new listening verdict. Automated pitch, audio and notation checks do not establish that the melodies sound good. The original review is retained in the score comments and the archived versions.
