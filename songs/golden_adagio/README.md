# Golden Adagio - Beyond the Veil

A five-minute orchestral piece developed from the user's chosen **B, Forward Lilt**. Western harmony occupies 75% of the scored duration; golden sevenths and ninths occupy 25%, distributed throughout. The illustrated player shows each instrument's performed notes, a compact current-bar timeline, and the chord-following binaural pairs.

Open the [illustrated player](http://127.0.0.1:56855/songs/golden_adagio/) or the [editable score in SongMap Studio](http://127.0.0.1:8865/?song=songs%2Fgolden_adagio%2Fbeyond_the_veil.sm).

`beyond_the_veil.sm` contains 96 bars at 80 BPM in 36-ET, followed by a twelve-second tail: exactly 300 seconds. `tools/compose_beyond_the_veil.py` transcribes the authored full-form notes and expression for each of the eight parts. It checks bar arithmetic and expands explicitly chosen voicings; it does not invent the music or alter the audio engine. The first eight violin bars preserve the selected B's pitches and rhythms.

The key route is D minor, F major, A-flat major, a D-minor memory, A major, and D major. Violin carries the principal line; cello takes it at 2:24. The major return begins at 3:12 and the broad apex at 4:00. Golden altered tones replace their nearby Western counterparts during the golden bar, then resolve into a familiar chord. Ordinary semitone suspensions and the internal close relationships of seventh/ninth harmony are retained.

Seven recorded instruments are used within their sample ranges: violins, cello, bowed double bass, flute, horn, folk harp and grand piano. A restrained synthesized organ extends the chapel passages. The supporting cello, winds and organ were lowered after the first ensemble balance check. Each instrument has its own note and expression pass, entrances, rests and developing role. This original melody responds to the requested broad string adagio character without quoting an existing work.

The binaural layer follows all 96 chords, Western as well as golden. Four or five audible low centers match current chord tones actually played in the bar. They are octave-folded pitch classes: most reduce upper notes, while a bass-only root can be represented above its fundamental in its harmonic octave class. Every beat difference is a further five-octave reduction of its own center: `difference = carrier_hz / 32`, configured as `beat=octave octaves=5`. The divisor stays constant through all chords and keys; no independent fixed-Hz ladder or section-rate ramps remain. The differences span 4.0879–15.7339 Hz. The level is 15 LU below the instrumental bed, with short chord-change crossfades and a final fade.
