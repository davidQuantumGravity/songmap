# Rational and Irrational Harmony

Exploratory concept for a two-volume textbook set, recorded 2026-10-25.

*Rational and Irrational Harmony* will study music through rhythm, pitch, tuning, and harmony, with mathematics connecting the subjects throughout. The set will combine a history of music theory across cultural traditions with listening, worked examples, and practical composition.

| Volume | Title | Central scope |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Rational Harmony* | A history of music theory organized around rhythm, notes, and harmony, including rational ratios and the many strategies for approximating and tempering them. |
| 2 | *Irrational Harmony* | A systematic exploration of musically useful irrational numbers and the systems they can generate, showing how the golden harmonic system fits into this broader field. |

## Volume 1 Rational Harmony

### Premise and teaching sequence

Begin with rhythm, develop notes and intervals, then build harmony. Use polyrhythms as a central connection between rhythmic and harmonic ratios. Return to that connection as the history, mathematics, and musical examples become more detailed.

One proposed demonstration starts with two regular pulse trains at three and two pulses per second. Increasing both rates by the same factor preserves their 3:2 relationship. At audio rates, corresponding periodic tones can express that same ratio as an interval. Three rates in the proportion 4:5:6 provide a further bridge from a rhythmic pattern to the frequency ratios of a just major triad. Explain the roles of waveform, spectrum, and perception alongside the arithmetic.

This progression is a teaching structure. Historical chapters should establish their own chronologies and connections through sources.

### Historical and cultural scope

The ambition is to engage every cultural tradition as far as the project can responsibly develop. Begin with rhythm and selected African traditions, treating named musical cultures and their practices individually. Extend the account across other world traditions and into ancient Greek theory, the medieval period, the Renaissance, and later developments.

Develop the detailed coverage through research into African, Middle Eastern, South Asian, East and Southeast Asian, Indigenous, European, and diasporic traditions, among others. Give each tradition its own terminology, performance practices, instruments, and purposes for organizing sound. Use mathematical comparisons while preserving distinctions between local concepts.

The historical programme should include transmission, exchange, oral practice, written theory, and differences between theoretical prescriptions and performed music. Treat the period sometimes called the “Dark Ages” through specific medieval contexts. Follow concurrent histories where a single chronological sequence would obscure them.

### Proposed organization

| Part | Subjects to explore |
|---|---|
| Rhythm and number | Pulse, duration, subdivision, meter, cyclic patterns, cross-rhythm, polyrhythm, and relations between rates. |
| From rhythm to notes | Periodic motion, frequency, waveform, spectrum, pitch, octave relations, and the rhythmic interpretation of frequency ratios. |
| Musical traditions and early theory | Selected African and other world traditions; ancient Greek theory; the relation between practical music, instruments, notation, and mathematical accounts. |
| Medieval and Renaissance developments | Modal practices, chant, polyphony, counterpoint, tuning, and the changing roles of intervals and simultaneous voices. |
| Rational tuning and its extensions | Pythagorean constructions, just intonation, prime limits, harmonic and subharmonic constructions, ratio lattices, and interval complexity. |
| Temperament and microtonal strategies | Commas, meantone and other temperaments, equal divisions, unequal divisions, rational approximation, and adaptive tuning. Include explicit studies of 12-EDO and 37-EDO. |
| Historical and contemporary harmony | Baroque, Classical, Romantic, and modern classical approaches; blues, jazz, pop, rhythm and blues (R&B), and related traditions, with attention to their own histories and rhythmic practices. |
| Comparison and composition | Compare systems using worked mathematics, listening examples, voice leading, rhythm, timbre, and complete musical passages. |

EDO means equal divisions of the octave. The tuning survey should aim to cover every practical strategy for microtonal harmony with rational approximations, using families of methods and worked comparisons. Further EDOs and tuning systems can be added as the research develops.

### Mathematical approach

Build the mathematics progressively: integer ratios, common cycles, frequency and period, logarithms and cents, prime factorization, continued fractions, approximation error, lattices, and temperament mappings. Define each quantity and symbol at first use. Connect each calculation to something the reader can hear, perform, or compose.

For a frequency ratio r, its interval size in cents is c = 1200 log₂(r). For N-EDO, the ratio at k steps is r = 2^(k/N), where N is the number of equal steps per octave and k is an integer. These formulas separate a rational frequency ratio, its logarithmic measurement, and an equal-tempered approximation.

The title *Rational Harmony* describes the organizing emphasis on ratios and their approximations. Equal temperament belongs in this volume even though many of its exact frequency ratios are irrational. Likewise, rational models should be identified as models wherever a tradition's practice requires a more nuanced account.

## Volume 2 Irrational Harmony

### Scope

Explore “all possible irrational numbers (within reason)” as foundations for musical relationships. Organize this ambition through representative families, explicit selection criteria, and reproducible searches. Study how an irrational number can serve as an interval ratio, a scale generator, a relationship among spectral components, or a relationship among rhythmic rates.

Candidate families include square roots and other radicals, the golden ratio and other metallic means, further algebraic numbers, and selected transcendental numbers such as e and π. A candidate's musical interest should emerge from its constructions and listening examples. Mathematical distinction alone does not establish musical usefulness.

### Proposed lines of exploration

- Generate scales by repeatedly applying an interval and reducing the results into an octave or another chosen period. Compare finite closure, near closure, and continuing chains.
- Build chords, melodic vocabularies, voice-leading methods, and modulation strategies from selected generators. Explore mixtures of generators as well as systems built from one number.
- Compare exact irrational targets with rational approximations and equal-tempered realizations. State the approximation error and the musical purpose of each realization.
- Extend the rhythm-to-harmony connection to irrational rate relationships. Examine finite performance realizations and their differences from the exact mathematical model.
- Explore spectra whose component frequencies follow irrational relationships, and investigate how the choice of timbre affects the resulting music.
- Compare systems through interval structure, scale organization, beating, harmonic context, playability, and listening. Keep claims about preference or emotion tied to the evidence supporting them.

Distinguish an irrational frequency ratio r from an irrational octave coordinate log₂(r). For example, the ratio √2 is irrational but occupies exactly half an octave. This distinction matters when comparing generated scales and equal temperaments across the two volumes.

### The golden harmonic system within the wider theory

Develop the golden harmonic system as a substantial case study within the broader exploration. Define its generator as φ = (1 + √5)/2, then study interval chains, octave reduction, golden thirds, chords, scales, voice leading, and modulation.

Compare exact golden constructions with their rational approximations and equal-tempered realizations, including the existing SongMap work in 36-EDO. Establish which properties follow from the golden ratio specifically, which belong to a wider family of irrational generators, and which depend on the selected temperament or compositional practice.

## Shared method and next exploration

Pair historical and theoretical discussion with listening examples, rhythmic demonstrations, explicit derivations, and editable musical examples. The current textbook's “Hear it,” “Understand it,” and “Derive it” layers offer a possible teaching format for the set.

The next development step is a research outline for both volumes: a map of traditions and source material, a taxonomy of rational and irrational constructions, and a small set of paired rhythm-and-harmony demonstrations. Use that work to establish the chapter structure and identify material that can be adapted from the existing golden-harmony textbook.
