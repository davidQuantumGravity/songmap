# Combine repetition and continuous development

Choose a writing method per layer rather than declaring the whole ensemble a loop.
A stable groove may support a developing bass, a two-bar comp figure and a melody
written across the entire verse. The containing verse–chorus group may itself recur.

Record three things for every layer:

- Its source unit and length: one-bar beat, two-bar accompaniment, eight-bar melody.
- Its phase at the parent boundary: restart, continue or align to a named grid.
- Its return policy and protected properties: reuse, bounded variation or new occurrence.

Let repeated accompaniment provide recognition while the lead changes destination,
contour, rests and response. A new lead does not require a new bass groove. Preserve
bass lines, harmony and comping the user praised unless changing them serves the
current authorized revision.

On a returning chorus, keep the hook and develop an ending, counterline, register
or entrance. On a later verse, reuse some rhythmic identity while writing a new
melodic sentence. A through-composed bridge can interrupt the larger cycle once.
Plan the bridge's return into the next chorus as carefully as the bridge itself.

Check dependencies when harmony changes. Relative notes and chord-following parts
may need new voicings even when their source unit is unchanged. Absolute protected
melodies may instead constrain the harmony. Do not relabel a changed pitch sequence
as unchanged simply because it came from the same motif name.

Treat main arc, section arc and local phrase arc separately. Reserve enough register,
density and loudness headroom for the intended climax. Sparse or exact repeated
material can be the strongest choice when it gives the foreground a clear role.

Audition the selected occurrence with its neighbors. For a shared edit, inspect
all materially different harmonic contexts and the final return, not just the first
example. Report reused writing, distinct occurrences and any structural changes.
