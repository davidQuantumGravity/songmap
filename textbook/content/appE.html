Terms

Every technical term of the book, in alphabetical order, with a short definition and a link to the section where it is introduced or defined. Symbols and spellings are listed in Appendix B; the status words theorem, measurement and hypothesis are used as in How to read this book. Golden-system terms that also have a Western meaning are defined for both.

0–9

12-ET (12-tone equal temperament)
The standard tuning: the octave divided into twelve equal semitones of ratio $2^{1/12}$, 100 cents each. §1.4 The octave
13-ET
The octave in 13 equal steps of 92.31 c. It tempers out the golden comma, so golden13 becomes perfectly even: the golden analogue of 12-ET. It contains no Western interval except the octave. §24.5 13-ET and 36-ET
36-comma
The amount by which 36 golden sixths miss 25 octaves: $\varphi^{36}/2^{25}$, −8.75 c. 36-ET tempers it out, so its golden chain closes into a circle, as 53-ET closes the chain of fifths. §24.4 Why 36
36-ET
36-tone equal temperament: the octave in 36 sixth-steps of 33.33 c. It contains 12-ET exactly (every third step) and renders the golden sixth within 0.24 c. Written 36edo in code. §24.1 Sixth-steps
36-tone row
An ordering of all 36 pitch classes of 36-ET. The golden chain $j \mapsto 25j$ is one, with every step a golden sixth and every three consecutive notes a golden triad. §33.4 36-tone rows and the golden chain
72 keys
The major and minor keys of 36-ET: 36 tonics, each with a major and a minor key. With the 72 golden scales (36 golden13, 36 silver13) they make the 144 tonal fields of the Well-Tempered Golden Clavier. §24.6 The key system: 72 keys and 72 golden scales

A

additive synthesis
Making a tone by adding sine waves (partials) of chosen frequencies and amplitudes; the method behind every instrument in the book's examples. §37.1 Additive synthesis
admissibility
The condition for a strict passing chord to be golden: the voice leading must have one voice of each motion class (hold, rising, falling), because a golden triad has one note on each layer. §27.2 The uniform path
aggregate
A collection containing all twelve pitch classes, each once, such as the two hexachords of a combinatorial row pair. §21.3 Combinatoriality
all-interval row
A twelve-tone row whose eleven successive intervals are 1, 2, …, 11 in some order. Its first and last notes are always a tritone apart. §21.4 All-interval rows
alteration list
A parenthesis after a chord symbol that bends named chord tones by sixth-steps: C(v3,^5) is C major with its third down and its fifth up, which is Cg. §25.6 Naming chords in 36-ET
amplitude
How far a vibration swings from its middle position; larger amplitude means a louder sound. §1.1 Vibration and waves
antecedent and consequent
The two phrases of a period: the first ends open (often on a half cadence), the second answers it and closes on the tonic. §13.5 Phrases and cadences in melody
antiphonal
Singing in which two half-choirs alternate; an antiphon is a short chant framing a psalm. §15.4 Call and response
apotome
The larger Pythagorean semitone, $3^7/2^{11} = 2187/2048$ (113.69 c), the chromatic step between a letter and its sharp, as F–F♯. §5.3 Two semitones: limma and apotome
appoggiatura
An accented, unprepared dissonance: approached by leap onto the beat and resolved by step. §12.3 Suspensions and appoggiaturas
asymmetry theorem
No uniform path goes golden → Western → golden: rational chords cannot be passing chords between golden homes, so golden homes move by their own operations (circle steps, planing, breathing). A theorem, checked exhaustively. §31.1 Why golden homes move differently
auditory scene analysis
The ear's grouping of partials into sources and streams, by harmonicity, common onset, common change and similarity. §17.2 Blend and contrast
augmentation
Playing a motif with every duration lengthened, usually doubled; diminution shortens them. §13.4 Inversion, retrograde, augmentation
averaging principle
A new chord can be formed as the voice-by-voice average of Western chords. Three triads always average onto the 36-ET grid: Cg = (C + Cm + Caug)/3 and Cs = (C + Cm + Cdim)/3. §25.3 The averaging principle
axes (Lendvai)
Ernő Lendvai's reading of Bartók's tonality: three diminished-seventh sets of roots, each treated as one function (tonic, dominant, subdominant). An analytical interpretation. §22.2 Bartók and Stravinsky

B

beat spectrum
The set of beat rates a chord produces from its nearly coinciding partials. A close-voiced golden seventh on harmonic tones beats at $f_0\varphi^{-6}\times(1, 2/\varphi, 4/\varphi^2)$ and $2f_0\varphi^{-6}$ (a measurement that depends on the timbre). §26.4 The beats of a golden chord
beats
The regular swelling and fading heard when two tones of nearly equal frequency sound together; the beat rate is the difference of the frequencies. §3.1 Beats
binaural beat
A beat heard when each ear receives a steady tone and the two differ slightly in frequency. It exists only in the brain's comparison of the ears, not in either sound wave. §34.1 Binaural beats and entrainment
Binet's formula
$F_n = (\varphi^n - \psi^n)/\sqrt5$, a closed formula for the Fibonacci numbers, with $\psi = -1/\varphi$. §23.1 The golden ratio
bliss conjecture
The author's hypothesis that sound golden at several scales (pitches, beat rates and timbre) and free of steady rational pulse tends to be experienced as calmer and more absorbing than matched rational sound. A hypothesis to be tested, not an established effect. §34.4 The bliss conjecture
breathing
Moving a golden home by adding the next chain note (Cg becomes Cg7) and then dropping the oldest (leaving vEg): one step of the golden circle in two stages. §31.3 Breathing and planing
bronze triad (T13)
The equal-third triad C ^E vA (0, 13, 26), golden reflected through augmented. It is the usual non-golden passing chord beside a golden one on strict paths; its third is near 9/7. Symbol CT13. §25.4 The equal-third line: silver, golden, bronze

C

cadence
The chords that close a phrase: authentic (V–I), half (ending on V), plagal (IV–I) and deceptive (V–vi). §10.4 Cadences
cadential six-four
A tonic triad in second inversion over the dominant bass, resolving to V; its golden counterpart is the golden six-four. §9.2 Inversions and voicing
call and response
Alternation between a leading voice or group and an answering one, found in worship, work and dance worldwide and in gospel, blues and jazz. §15.4 Call and response
canon
Strict imitation in which a follower repeats the leader's whole line at a fixed time and pitch interval; a round is a canon at the unison or octave that can repeat indefinitely. §15.2 Imitation and canon
cantus firmus
The given melody, in long equal notes, against which counterpoint is written in species exercises. §15.1 Species counterpoint
cent
The book's unit of interval size: 1/1200 of an octave. A ratio $r$ measures $1200\log_2 r$ cents. §1.5 Measuring intervals in cents
centroid
The average of several points of chord space. Cg is the centroid of C, Cm and Caug; the augmented triad lies at the centre of three-note chord space. §14.4 The geometry of voice leading
chain distance
The number of golden sixths between two notes of the golden chain, the difference of their chain positions $j$. Every interval of a golden scale is a chain distance folded into the octave. §23.3 The golden chain
chain of fifths
The endless line of notes a pure fifth apart, F C G D A E B F♯ …; in Pythagorean tuning it never closes. §5.2 The chain of fifths
chord
Several notes sounding together, named by root, quality and bass. §9.1 Triads
chromatic harmony
Harmony using notes outside the key without leaving it, typically moving voices by semitones. §18.1 Chromaticism inside tonality
chromatic mediant
A chord a major or minor third from the tonic with the same quality, such as E, A♭, A or E♭ major in C. All of them have strict golden passing chords to the tonic. §11.5 Chromatic mediants
circle progression
A progression whose roots fall by fifths, so that each chord acts like a dominant of the next. §11.1 Circle progressions
clean master
SongMap's master setting for the book: the mix is scaled so its highest peak is at −1 dBFS, with no equalization, compression, limiting or saturation. §37.4 Mixing for clarity
clean tone
A synthesized tone with no vibrato, drift, detuning, noise, filter or saturation: every partial an exact sine at a fixed frequency, so any beating comes from the tuning alone. §37.2 Clean, stable tones
closely related keys
Keys whose signatures differ by at most one accidental: for C major, G and F major and the relative minors A, E and D minor. §8.4 Near and far keys
combination tone
A faint extra pitch produced in the ear by two loud tones, chiefly at $f_2 - f_1$ and $2f_1 - f_2$. For the exact golden chain voicing every such tone lands on the golden chain. §26.3 Combination tones that land on the chain
comma
A small interval measuring the mismatch between two routes to "the same" note, such as the Pythagorean comma (23.46 c) or the syntonic comma (21.51 c). §4.5 Small gaps: a first look at commas
comma pump
A progression of just intervals that returns to its starting chord displaced by a comma, showing that just intonation cannot keep a fixed pitch set. §4.5 Small gaps: a first look at commas
consonance tiers
The book's proposed classification of intervals for golden-spectrum voices: golden perfect (unison, octave, golden sixth and third), golden imperfect, mild and sharp dissonance. Based on measured roughness; a compositional proposal. §30.3 Counterpoint with golden voices
continued fraction
A number written as $a_0 + 1/(a_1 + 1/(a_2 + \cdots))$, $[a_0; a_1, a_2, \ldots]$. Its truncations, the convergents, are its best rational approximations. §5.5 Continued fractions and why twelve
contour
The pattern of rises and falls of a melody, independent of exact intervals. §13.1 Contour
convergent
A fraction obtained by stopping a continued fraction early. The convergents of $\log_2(3/2)$ give 12 fifths ≈ 7 octaves; those of $\log_2\varphi$ give 13 golden sixths ≈ 9 octaves and 36 ≈ 25. §5.5 Continued fractions and why twelve
coset
A subgroup shifted by a fixed element; the cosets of a subgroup tile the group. The augmented triads are the cosets of {0, 4, 8}, and the layers of 36-ET are the cosets of 12-ET. §19.4 Subgroups and cosets of Z12
counterpoint
The art of combining independent melodic lines. §15.1 Species counterpoint
countersubject
A line in a fugue that regularly accompanies the subject or answer. §15.3 Fugue
crest factor
The ratio of a signal's peak to its root-mean-square level, in decibels; a single sine has 3.01 dB. §37.2 Clean, stable tones
critical band
The frequency range within which two pure tones interact in the inner ear and produce roughness; it widens with frequency. §3.2 Roughness and the critical band
cue sheet
The cues.json file SongMap writes with every render: sections, events and the time and video frame of every beat and bar. §38.4 Tablature and animation
cyclic group
A group in which every element is a multiple (or power) of one generator, as $\mathbb{Z}_{12}$ is generated by 1 or by 7. §8.1 Pitch classes and Z12

D

decibel
The logarithmic unit of level: $20\log_{10}$ of an amplitude ratio. Doubling the amplitude adds about 6 dB. §1.2 Frequency, pitch and loudness
diaschisma
The comma 2048/2025 (19.55 c) between the just tritones 64/45 and 45/32. §4.5 Small gaps: a first look at commas
diatonic triads
The triads built on the seven degrees of a major or minor scale, named by Roman numerals. §9.1 Triads
dictionary of transitions
The generated table of four-part realizations of all 870 ordered pairs among 30 chords in C major, with their golden passing chords under System 1 and System 2. Its counts are exact for its voicing model, not theorems about all voicings. §28.4 The dictionary of transitions
diesis
The comma 128/125 (41.06 c) by which three just major thirds fall short of an octave. §7.1 The syntonic comma and meantone
dissonance curve
Computed roughness of a timbre against interval size; its dips mark the intervals at which that timbre's partials line up. §17.3 Timbre shapes harmony
dominant
The fifth degree of a key and the triad on it (V), which pulls toward the tonic. §10.1 Home: the tonic

E

EEG rhythms
Voltage oscillations recorded at the scalp, conventionally sorted into frequency bands (delta, theta, alpha, beta, gamma) whose boundaries vary between laboratories. §34.2 The golden-ratio EEG hypothesis
enharmonic
Two names for the same 12-ET pitch, such as C♯ and D♭; in Pythagorean tuning they are different pitches a comma apart. §8.3 Keys and key signatures
entrainment
The popular claim that a rhythmic stimulus such as a binaural beat pulls brain rhythms toward its rate; the evidence for it is mixed. §34.1 Binaural beats and entrainment
envelope
How a sound's loudness evolves: its attack, hold and decay. §2.4 Timbre as a spectrum
equal temperament (n-ET, EDO)
A tuning with the octave divided into $n$ equal steps of $1200/n$ cents. 12-ET was computed by Zhu Zaiyu (1584) and Simon Stevin (c. 1605) and popularized by Mersenne (1636). §7.3 Equal temperament
equal-third line
The family $T_n = \{0, n, 2n\}$ of triads built from two equal thirds of $n$ sixth-steps: T8, diminished (9), silver (10), golden (11), augmented (12) and bronze (13), evenly spaced on one straight line of chord space. §25.4 The equal-third line: silver, golden, bronze
equal-third triad
A triad of two equal stacked thirds, $T_n = \{0, n, 2n\}$. The partner theorem says the non-golden passing chord on every Western-ended golden path is one: bronze or silver. §27.5 Never twice, and the bronze partner
equivalence relation
A relation that is reflexive, symmetric and transitive; octave equivalence is one, and its classes are the pitch classes. §1.4 The octave
equivalent rectangular bandwidth (ERB)
Glasberg and Moore's measure of the critical band, $24.7\,(4.37f/1000 + 1)$ Hz. §3.2 Roughness and the critical band
Euclidean rhythm
A rhythm that spreads $k$ onsets as evenly as possible over $n$ pulses, such as the tresillo (3 in 8). §16.1 Pulse and meter
Euler's gradus
Leonhard Euler's 1739 measure of the simplicity of a ratio, computed from the prime factors of its least common multiple; lower is sweeter. §3.3 Harmonicity and virtual pitch
evenness
How close a chord is to dividing the octave into equal parts; the augmented triad is perfectly even among triads. §9.4 Chord space
exposition (fugue)
The opening of a fugue, in which the voices enter one by one with the subject or answer. §15.3 Fugue
extensions
Ninths, elevenths and thirteenths: chord tones stacked in thirds above the seventh. §9.3 Seventh chords and beyond

F

fauxbourdon
A 15th-century texture: a melody with a voice a sixth below and an unwritten middle voice a fourth below, giving chains of sixth-three chords. §35.2 The arrival of thirds
Fibonacci numbers
The sequence 0, 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 21, …, each the sum of the two before. Ratios of neighbours approach φ, and $\varphi^n = F_n\varphi + F_{n-1}$. §23.1 The golden ratio
Fibonacci word
The never-repeating sequence L S L L S L S L L S …, made by replacing each L by LS and each S by L; as a rhythm it is as balanced as two note lengths allow. §16.4 Proportion and the golden section
figured bass
Numbers written above the bass that give the intervals of the chord above it, such as 6 for a first inversion and 6/4 for a second. §9.2 Inversions and voicing
finite check
An exhaustive computer enumeration of a finite, precisely defined set of cases. It is a proof, verified by rerunning code rather than by reading. §D.1 Tuning and scales
formant
A fixed band of spectral emphasis set by an instrument's body or the vocal tract, which stays in place as the pitch moves. §2.4 Timbre as a spectrum
four-part writing
Harmony in four voices, soprano, alto, tenor and bass (SATB), the classic setting for studying voice leading. §14.1 Four-part writing
four-path theorem
Every golden triad is the passing chord of exactly four stepwise uniform paths at each position, 288 ordered paths in all, falling into 12 types up to transposition and reversal. A theorem. §27.3 The four-path theorem
Fourier series
The representation of a periodic function as a sum of sines and cosines at whole multiples of its repetition rate. §2.3 Adding sines: Fourier's idea
frequency
The number of vibration cycles per second, measured in hertz; the reciprocal of the period. §1.1 Vibration and waves
fugue
A contrapuntal procedure built on the imitation of one theme, the subject, through all voices. §15.3 Fugue
fundamental
The lowest component of a harmonic tone, at the repetition rate of its vibration; it usually sets the perceived pitch. §1.2 Frequency, pitch and loudness

G

generator
An element whose multiples reach every element of a cyclic group; in tuning, the interval that is stacked to build a scale (the fifth, the golden sixth). §8.2 The circle as an orbit
generic and specific intervals
An interval counted in scale steps (a "third") is generic; its exact size in cents is specific. §6.2 Moments of symmetry
Gibbs phenomenon
The overshoot of about 9 percent of a jump that a truncated Fourier series shows next to any discontinuity, however many terms are taken. §2.3 Adding sines: Fourier's idea
golden Andalusian
The descending Andalusian progression vi – ♭VIg – v♭VIg – V – ^♯IVg – v♯IVg – IV – III with golden chords on ♭VI and ♯IV, ending in sub-chromatic planing into the Phrygian cadence. §29.5 The golden staircase and the Andalusian
golden apotome
The larger step of golden13, $\varphi^3/4$ (99.27 c): three golden sixths minus two octaves. 36-ET renders it as 3 sixth-steps. §23.4 The golden comma, apotome and limma
golden authentic cadence
V – vIg/V – I, or V – v♭IIIT13/V – vIg/V – I: in C, G – vE♭T13/G – vCg/G – C, the golden six-four inside a cadence. §29.1 The golden authentic cadence
golden beat ladder
A set of binaural beat rates in golden ratio, $B, B/\varphi, B/\varphi^2$ (for example 10, 6.18, 3.82 Hz), each the difference of the two above it. §34.1 Binaural beats and entrainment
golden chain
The sequence of notes a golden sixth apart, chain note $j$ at $1200\{j\log_2\varphi\}$ cents; in 36-ET it is the 36-tone row $25j \bmod 36$. §23.3 The golden chain
golden circle
The cycle of all 36 golden triads in which each step moves one voice by a semitone and neighbours share two notes and together form a golden seventh: Cg → vEg → ^Gg → Bg → …. §31.2 The golden circle
golden clausula
The proposed closing formula of golden counterpoint: C♯ and B, the notes that enclose the tonic in golden7, moving by golden apotomes to C. §30.3 Counterpoint with golden voices
golden comma
The overshoot of thirteen golden sixths over nine octaves, $\varphi^{13}/2^9$ (30.17 c); also golden apotome minus golden limma. One sixth-step in 36-ET. §23.4 The golden comma, apotome and limma
golden deceptive cadence
V – v♭VIg – ♭VIg – vi: in C, G – vA♭g – A♭g – Am, two golden chords that foreshadow ♭VI. §29.3 The golden deceptive cadence
golden escalator
Two golden chords in a row on a natural path between triads whose roots are a whole step apart, placed on the chromatic root between them: F – vF♯g – ^F♯g – G. §28.2 The golden escalator
golden fifth
Two stacked golden thirds, $4/\varphi^2$ (733.82 c), a fifth a little wider than pure; 22 sixth-steps (^P5). §23.2 Golden intervals
golden home
The author's model of the golden triad as a local minimum of tension: a delicate, metastable home one can dwell in and return to, while C major remains the global minimum and ultimate release. A model and listening experience to be tested; standard roughness and entropy models on harmonic tones do not show such a minimum. §31.1 Why golden homes move differently
golden intervals
The ratios $2^m\varphi^n$ with whole numbers $m, n$: the octaves and golden sixths and everything stacked from them. §23.2 Golden intervals
golden key distance
The closeness of two golden scales, measured along the golden chain: golden13 scales whose tonics are $j$ chain links apart share $\max(0, 13 - |j|)$ notes. §24.6 The key system: 72 keys and 72 golden scales
golden limma
The smaller step of golden13, $2^7/\varphi^{10}$ (69.10 c): seven octaves minus ten golden sixths. 36-ET renders it as 2 sixth-steps. §23.4 The golden comma, apotome and limma
golden mediant neighbour
I – Ig – ♭VI – Ig – I (C – Cg – A♭ – Cg – C): the golden chord as a neighbour between the tonic and its chromatic mediant. §29.4 Mediant gold
golden modal mixture
I – Ig – i (C – Cg – Cm): the golden triad, the centroid of major, minor and augmented, as a pivot between parallel major and minor. §29.6 Modal mixture and the golden Picardy
golden Picardy
i – I(^m3) – Ig – I (Cm – Cm(^3) – Cg – C): the third climbs 9, 10, 11, 12 sixth-steps from minor to major. §29.6 Modal mixture and the golden Picardy
golden pivot
A golden chord used to move between keys on different 36-ET layers, as in C → Cg → ^C; a golden triad shares at most one note with any key on a single layer. §32.2 Golden pivot chords
golden plagal glow
I – vIVg – IV and its reverse, with the golden chord vFg between C and F; the golden plagal amen is IV – vIVg/IV – I. §29.2 Golden plagal glow
golden Pythagorean scale
The 13-note scale made, like Pythagoras' 12-note scale, by stacking a generator (the golden sixth φ instead of the fifth) and folding into the octave until the chain nearly closes, overshooting by the golden comma; golden13. Preface, A scale from the golden ratio
golden ratio φ
$\varphi = (1+\sqrt5)/2 = 1.6180339887$, the positive root of $x^2 = x + 1$. As an interval it is the golden sixth, 833.09 c. §23.1 The golden ratio
golden scales
Scales made of consecutive notes of the golden chain. The well-formed ones have 2, 3, 4, 7, 10, 13, 23 or 36 notes; SongMap knows golden3, golden4, golden7, golden10, golden13, golden23 and silver13. §23.5 Well-formed golden scales
golden section
The division of a length so that the whole is to the larger part as the larger part is to the smaller; the larger part is $1/\varphi \approx 0.618$ of the whole. §16.4 Proportion and the golden section
golden serialism
Serial composition with 36-tone rows, especially rows built from the golden chain and transformed by the units of $\mathbb{Z}_{36}$. Experimental, proposed in this book. §33.5 Golden serialism
golden seventh
The chord Cg7 = C vE ^G B (0, 11, 22, 33), three stacked golden thirds, exactly $1 : 2/\varphi : 4/\varphi^2 : 8/\varphi^3$. Its top interval $8/\varphi^3$ (1100.73 c) is the golden seventh, rendered in 36-ET as the 12-ET major seventh. §25.2 The golden seventh
golden six-four
The golden triad a sixth-step below the tonic over the dominant bass, vCg/G, reached from G with the leading tone rising B → ^B → vC → C. The golden counterpart of the cadential six-four. §27.6 Worked example: the golden six-four
golden sixth
The interval φ (833.09 c), the generator of the golden chain; 25 sixth-steps in 36-ET (^m6). §23.2 Golden intervals
golden spectralism
Harmony made from the partials of golden spectra, in the manner of the spectral composers. Proposed in this book. §33.6 Golden spectralism
golden spectrum
A digital tone colour with partials at $2^m\varphi^n$ times the fundamental (1, 1.618, 2, 2.618, 3.236, 4, …). Physical instruments are harmonic; golden spectra exist only in synthesis, where golden chords measure as the smoothest chords. §26.2 Golden-spectrum timbres
golden staircase
A diatonic ladder in which each whole step is approached through two golden chromatic chords (a golden escalator), I – v♭IIg – ♭IIg – ii – …. §29.5 The golden staircase and the Andalusian
golden third
The interval $2/\varphi$ (366.91 c), between the minor and major thirds; the inversion of the golden sixth, 11 sixth-steps (vM3). §23.2 Golden intervals
golden tonal system
The book's system of golden-ratio harmony in 36-ET: golden intervals, chords and scales, used beside Western harmony. Preface, Why now: exploring with a machine
golden transform M11
Multiplication of 36-ET pitch classes by 11. It maps the sixth-step cluster {0, 1, 2} to the golden triad {0, 11, 22} and has order 6. §33.2 The golden transform M11
golden triad
The chord Cg = C vE ^G (0, 11, 22), two stacked golden thirds, exactly $1 : 2/\varphi : 4/\varphi^2$. With harmonic tones it measures rougher than major, minor and augmented but smoother than diminished. §25.1 The golden triad
golden-ratio EEG hypothesis
The hypothesis (Pletzer, Kerschbaum and Klimesch, 2010) that the centre frequencies of neighbouring EEG bands relate by a ratio near φ, which would minimize spurious synchronization. A published hypothesis, not an established fact. §34.2 The golden-ratio EEG hypothesis
golden13
The thirteen-note golden scale C C♯ D vE♭ vE vF vF♯ ^F♯ ^G ^A♭ ^A B♭ B: ten golden apotomes and three golden limmas, the golden "chromatic". §23.7 golden13 and silver13
golden7
The seven-note golden scale C C♯ vE vF ^G ^A♭ B (steps 3 8 3 8 3 8 3 sixth-steps), the golden counterpart of the diatonic. It holds five golden triads, four golden sevenths and no Western triad. §23.6 golden7: the golden diatonic

H

half cadence
A phrase ending on the dominant, which sounds open. §13.5 Phrases and cadences in melody
half-step theorem
Chords a half step apart in parallel motion can never pass through a golden chord; their passing chords are planing. An exhaustive check found no golden passing chord between major or minor triads a half step apart under either system. §28.3 The half-step theorem
harmonic entropy
Paul Erlich's measure of how ambiguous an interval is: how many simple ratios compete to explain it once hearing's imprecision is taken into account. A model. §3.4 Harmonic entropy
harmonic function
The role of a chord in a key: tonic (home), dominant (pull) or subdominant (departure). §10.1 Home: the tonic
harmonic minor
The minor scale with a raised seventh degree, giving a leading tone and a major dominant. §8.5 Relative and parallel keys
harmonic series
The frequencies $f_1, 2f_1, 3f_1, \ldots$ of the partials of an ideal string or air column; its intervals include 2:1, 3:2, 5:4 and 7:4. §2.2 Partials and the harmonic series
harmonic seventh chord
The chord 4:5:6:7 of harmonics 4 to 7, a dominant seventh with the seventh tuned to 7/4 (968.83 c). §2.5 Intervals hidden in the series
harmonicity
The degree to which the notes of an interval or chord fit a single harmonic series with a fundamental that is not too low. §3.3 Harmonicity and virtual pitch
hemiola
Three beats of two felt across two beats of three, as when two bars of 3/4 sound like one bar of 3/2. §16.1 Pulse and meter
hertz (Hz)
The unit of frequency: cycles per second. §1.1 Vibration and waves
hexachord theorem
Milton Babbitt's theorem: a six-note set and its complement have the same interval vector. §20.4 Interval vectors
hexachordal combinatoriality
The property of a row whose first hexachord, under some transformation, becomes the complement of itself, so that two row forms together make aggregates. §21.3 Combinatoriality
hexatonic and octatonic cycles
Cycles of triads made by alternating two neo-Riemannian moves: P and L give six triads on six pitch classes, P and R eight triads on an octatonic set. §10.5 Neo-Riemannian moves and the Tonnetz
hexatonic pole
Two triads at opposite ends of a hexatonic cycle, such as C major and A♭ minor: as far apart as the system allows, yet only three semitones of motion apart. §18.3 Hexatonic and octatonic cycles
hidden fifths and octaves
Arrival at a perfect fifth or octave by similar motion, from a different interval; also called direct. §14.2 The classical rules and their reasons
Hurwitz's theorem
Every irrational number has infinitely many fractions $p/q$ within $1/(\sqrt5\,q^2)$ of it, and $\sqrt5$ cannot be improved because of φ (Hurwitz, 1891): φ is the number hardest to approximate by fractions. §23.1 The golden ratio
hypermeter
The grouping of bars into strong and weak pairs and four-bar units, as if each bar were one beat of a larger meter. §16.2 Period and sentence
hypothesis
