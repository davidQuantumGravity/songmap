Why golden homes move differently

In Parts VI and VII so far, Western chords were home and golden chords were guests: brief, luminous passing chords between diatonic harmonies (Chapter 27). Chapter 26 showed that timbre can turn this around. On golden-spectrum tones, whose partials are powers of two times powers of the golden ratio, the golden triad is the smoothest chord and Western triads are rough (Chapter 26). Then golden harmony can be home. This chapter asks how a golden home moves, and what plays the roles that tonic, dominant and cadence play in Western harmony (Chapter 10).

The first surprise is an asymmetry. Listen to the two halves of the next example.

In the rational home the golden chord sits naturally between two Western chords, one third of the way along the voice leading. In the golden home the Western chord is not on the way to anything. It is a visitor: the music leaves home, touches C major and returns.

Recall the uniform path of System 1. Every voice of a chord X moves by at most a whole step to a chord Y, and the motion is cut into three equal parts, so the two passing chords are $P_1 = (2X+Y)/3$ and $P_2 = (X+2Y)/3$, voice by voice. Between Western chords, these passing chords are golden in exactly the cases of the quality theorem (Chapter 27): for example C → A♭ passes through Cg.

Now invert the roles: let X and Y be golden triads and ask whether a passing chord can be Western. The answer is never. This is the asymmetry theorem of GOLDEN_THEORY.md §3.1, proved below. Counting all uniform paths between golden triads makes the picture complete (computed exhaustively):

Theorem (asymmetry). Let $X = (r, r+11, r+22)$ be a golden triad (voices root, third, fifth), let $d\in\{-2,\dots,2\}^3$, and let $P_k = X + k\,d$ for $k = 0,1,2,3$ (sixth-steps mod 36), with $Y = P_3$ golden. Then neither $P_1$ nor $P_2$ lies on a single layer. Status: theorem; also checked exhaustively in reference/golden/classify.py §G.

Proof. Write $e_1 = d_3 - d_1$ and $e_2 = d_5 - d_1$ (third and fifth displacement relative to the root), so $|e_i|\le 4$. The pairwise differences of a golden triad are, mod 36, in $\{\pm 11, \pm 22\} = \{11, 14, 22, 25\}$, in any voice order. In $Y$, the third minus the root is $11 + 3e_1\equiv 2 \pmod 3$; the only allowed values $\equiv 2$ are 11 and 14, so $3e_1\equiv 0$ or $3 \pmod{36}$, hence $e_1\in\{0, 1\}$. Likewise the fifth minus the root is $22+3e_2\equiv 1$, so it must be 22 or 25 and $e_2\in\{0,1\}$.

The layer of a pitch class is its residue mod 3, and the voices of $X$ sit on layers $\lambda, \lambda+2, \lambda+1$ (the layer theorem, Chapter 25). If $P_1$ is on one layer then $d_1 \equiv 2 + d_3 \equiv 1 + d_5 \pmod 3$, so $e_2 \equiv -1\equiv 2$, which contradicts $e_2\in\{0,1\}$. If $P_2$ is on one layer then $2d_1\equiv 2+2d_3 \pmod 3$; multiplying by 2 (its own inverse mod 3) gives $e_1\equiv -1 \equiv 2$, which contradicts $e_1\in\{0,1\}$. ∎

Corollary (planing). A uniform path between golden triads has golden passing chords exactly when it is parallel, $d = (k,k,k)$ with $k\in\{\pm1,\pm2\}$: $4\times 36 = 144$ paths, matching the exhaustive count. Proof. The constraints of the proof above leave $(e_1, e_2) \in \{(0,0), (0,1), (1,1)\}$ ($(1,0)$ would make fifth minus third $11 - 3 = 8$). For $(0,0)$ the path is parallel. For $(0,1)$ the passing chords have fifth minus root $22 + k$, and for $(1,1)$ third minus root $11 + k$ ($k = 1, 2$); neither value is in $\{11, 14, 22, 25\}$, so they are not golden. Counting $d_1 \in \{-2,\dots,2\}$ within range gives $5 + 4 + 4 = 13$ motion vectors per triad, 12 without the trivial $d = 0$: $4$ parallel and $8$ non-parallel, hence $36 \times 12 = 432 = 144 + 288$. ∎

The golden circle

The circle of fifths organizes Western keys: each step to a neighbour changes as little as possible, and twelve steps bring you home (Chapter 8). The golden home has its own circle, made of golden triads. Each step keeps two notes and moves the third by a semitone. Listen to thirteen chords (twelve steps), two beats each.

The golden circle moves the root up a golden third (11 sixth-steps) each time:

Cg → vEg → ^Gg → Bg → vE♭g → ^F♯g → B♭g → vDg → ^Fg → Ag → vC♯g → ^Eg → A♭g → …

Because 11 and 36 share no factor, the circle visits all 36 golden triads before it returns to Cg. Here are its roots on the 36-ET pitch circle; the path is a 36-pointed star.

Three properties make it a circle of nearest neighbours:

  • Two common tones. Cg = C vE ^G and vEg = vE ^G B share vE and ^G. The step moves one voice, C down to B, a semitone, on the same layer. In exact tuning that semitone is the golden apotome, 99.27 c.
  • Neighbours make the golden seventh. The union of two neighbours is a golden seventh chord: Cg ∪ vEg = C vE ^G B = Cg7. In the other direction, Cg ∪ ^A♭g = ^A♭ C vE ^G = ^A♭g7.
  • Chain windows. In chain voicing (the notes as consecutive golden-chain tones), a golden triad is a window of three consecutive chain notes and the circle step slides the window by one: the chain note at one end is replaced by the one three positions further on. This is the sliding window of GOLDEN_THEORY.md §3.2.

The Western mirror: the leading-tone exchange

The voice motion of a circle step is familiar. The neo-Riemannian move L, the leading-tone exchange, takes C major to E minor by lowering C to B and keeping E and G (Chapter 10). Cg → vEg is the same gesture: C falls to B, vE and ^G stay. In Western harmony, alternating L with R (the relative move) gives the chain of thirds C – Em – G – Bm – D – …, which visits all 24 major and minor triads in 24 steps. The golden circle needs only one move, because the golden triad is its own mirror image: there is no major/minor alternation, and a single L-like move, repeated, visits all 36 golden triads.

Proposition. (i) $G_r\cap G_{r+11} = \{r+11, r+22\}$. (ii) $G_r\cup G_{r+11} = \{r, r+11, r+22, r+33\}$, the golden seventh on $r$. After 36 steps the exact chain has moved 36 places, and since $\varphi^{36}/2^{25}$ is −8.75 c, the exact circle returns to C 8.75 c sharp. 36-ET closes it, exactly as 12-ET closes the circle of fifths over the Pythagorean comma.

Western check. Iterating L and R alternately from C major (L: C→Em, R: Em→G, …) returns to C major after 24 moves, having visited every major and minor triad once (computed).

Breathing and planing

Two more ways to move in a golden home. Breathing walks the circle in half-steps: the triad first gains the next chain tone (it inhales into a golden seventh), then lets its root go (it exhales into the next triad). Planing keeps the shape and slides the whole chord up a sixth-step at a time, so the chord changes color without changing kind.

Breathing

Breathing alternates triad and seventh: Cg → Cg7 → vEg → vEg7 → ^Gg. Adding B to Cg makes Cg7 (the union of two circle neighbours); dropping C leaves vEg. In chain terms the window grows from three tones to four and shrinks back to three, one chain step further on. It is the circle step taken in two halves, with the golden seventh as the in-breath. Since the golden seventh is the chord of φ³ (Chapter 25), breathing is also the gentlest way to bring the golden seventh's beats in and out (Chapter 26).

Planing and the layer prism

Planing adds one sixth-step to every voice. Three planings make a semitone transposition, Cg → C♯g. On the way, the chord passes through all three layer colorings. The layer theorem says a golden triad has one note on each layer; planing rotates which chord member sits on the 12-ET layer:

Planing in a golden home is the counterpart of the half-step theorem in a rational home (Chapter 28). There, parallel half-step motion between Western chords forced planing passing chords, Western chords shifted off the grid, and could never pass through golden. Here, parallel motion is the one way a golden chord can glide through golden passing chords to another golden chord (the corollary in the first section).

Rational visitation

In a golden home, Western chords become visitors. They arrive, color the harmony with their brighter, rougher sound (on golden-spectrum tones), and leave. Listen to Cg visited by four rational neighbours in turn.

The visitors are the Western triads nearest to Cg in voice-leading distance, measured in sixth-steps summed over the voices (Chapter 14). On the home layer they are C at distance 2, Cm and Caug at 3, and A♭ at 4 (explore.py §5). They are the same chords that made Cg in the first place: Cg is the average of C, Cm and Caug (Chapter 25), and it is the passing chord from C to A♭.

Searching all three layers gives a symmetric picture. Exactly three Western triads lie at distance 2 from Cg, one on each layer, and each keeps one note of Cg:

Visitation as the inverse of passing

The two worlds describe the same pair of chords from opposite sides. In a rational home, C → Cg → A♭ is a passage and C – Cg – C a neighbour motion (the golden mediant neighbour of GOLDEN_THEORY.md §4.2). In a golden home, Cg – C – Cg is a visit. Which reading is right is decided by timbre, that is, by which chord is acoustically at rest. With golden-spectrum overtones the roughness values are major 3.17, minor 3.26 and augmented 3.14 against 2.08 for the golden triad (Chapter 26; measurement), so the visitors really are the tension.

Because visitors cannot pass (the asymmetry theorem), a visit is always a departure and return, like a Western neighbour chord (I – IV⁶₄ – I) rather than a passing chord. That suggests treating visits the way Western practice treats neighbour chords: short, on weak beats, with the common tone held. Status: compositional suggestion.

Toward golden7 function

Western function names what chords do in a key: the tonic rests, the dominant pulls back to it, the subdominant departs (Chapter 10). Does golden7 have anything similar? Nobody knows yet. This section collects what can be proved about the golden7 chords and states one hypothesis, clearly marked. First, listen to each golden7 triad moving to Cg.

Status: open research. Which golden7 progressions feel functional, with tonic, predominant and dominant analogues, is an open question (GOLDEN_THEORY.md §6, question 2). The facts in this section are theorems about the scale; what they mean for the ear is a hypothesis to be tested by listening.

The sevenths are Cg7 (C vE ^G B), ^A♭g7, vFg7 and C♯g7. Within golden7 the voice-leading distance between two of its triads is exactly three sixth-steps per circle step. And golden7 itself is Cg ∪ C♯g ∪ {B}: the tonic triad, the triad a semitone above it, and the leading tone.

Two further facts point at C as the center:

  • The enclosing pair. Of golden7's seven "thirds" (two-degree intervals), six are golden thirds and one is defective: B up to C♯, 198.54 c (Chapter 30). Its two notes are the semitone neighbours of C, one golden apotome below and above. In the diatonic scale the single defective fifth, the tritone B–F, plays this role: it points to C. Among the golden7 triads, B belongs only to vEg and C♯ only to C♯g.
  • Symmetry. golden7 is symmetric about C (chain −3 to +3), so C is the scale's own center, unlike C major, whose center of symmetry is D.

One thing cannot create function: chord quality. All golden triads are transpositions of one shape and sound alike in isolation, in roughness, entropy and beats. That is also true of I, IV and V in a major key: function comes from the scale context, not from the chords themselves.

A hypothesis

GOLDEN_THEORY.md proposes ranking golden7 chords by golden-circle distance from Cg. Combining that with the facts above suggests a working map, offered only as a hypothesis for listening tests:

  • Home: Cg (and Cg7, which adds the leading tone B).
  • Rising side: vEg, the only triad containing B. Its arrival on Cg is the leading-tone exchange in reverse, B rising to C: a candidate for a dominant-like role.
  • Falling side: ^A♭g, vFg and C♯g, graded by how many voices fall a semitone into Cg (one, two, three). C♯g contains the upper leading tone C♯ and resolves by pure semitone descent in all voices, like a Phrygian or Neapolitan approach in Western harmony.

Concrete questions to test: Does vEg → Cg sound more conclusive than ^A♭g → Cg? Does C♯g → Cg sound like a cadence or like a slide? Does the order ^A♭g – vFg – C♯g – Cg build tension and release? The cadence example above is a first probe.

What is, and is not, "well tempered"

Bach's title refers to a well temperament, an unequal tuning in which every key is usable but keys differ in color. Bach did not invent equal temperament: Zhu Zaiyu (1584) and Simon Stevin (c. 1605) computed it, and Mersenne popularized it (1636). 36-ET is an equal temperament, so the golden clavier's keys do not differ in tuning color. The name is an homage to the idea of a complete tour of a tuning system. What the golden clavier shares with Bach's is the claim that every key of the system is usable, here including the 24 Western keys on each of the two off-grid layers and the golden and silver scales on all 36 tonics.
