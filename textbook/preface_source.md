# Author's preface draft (source text, by David Chester, 2026-10-21)

Years ago, I discovered that a new musical scale could be constructed from the golden ratio. Pythagoras noticed that (3/2)^12 was close to 2^7, building a 12-note scale. By interpreting phi = (1+sqrt(5))/2 as a "golden sixth", its octave inversion leads to a "golden third". I noticed that stacking two golden thirds led to a triad that sounded decent and mysterious, better than a diminished chord, perhaps even angelic. Furthermore, (phi)^13 ~ 2^9, which allows for a golden Pythagorean scale with 13 notes to be defined in the same manner.
Then, I realized that approximately, the golden third was inbetween a major and a minor chord by almost exactly 2/3 major and 1/3 minor. As such, just as Bach created 12 ET with a perfectly chromatic scale that approximates Pythagoras' musical scale, 36 ET approximates the 13-note golden Pythagorean scale. Not only can we now do key changes between different golden Pythagorean scales, all of these scales also unify with a tonal system that allows for three copies of standard 12 ET Western harmony.
For years, I lacked motivation to explore the system much further than a few basic MIDI files and a notebook scribbled with chord progressions that I had never heard. A whole new color palette of audio that I am conjecturing should sound "good" based on mathematics that has counterintuitive intuition. Rational notes sound good, so the most irrational number should sound bad! Music is based on symmetry, yet, the major chord asymmetrically stacks a major third with a minor third, and the most symmetric diminished chord is the most dissonant. The truth is more subtle, and perhaps golden tones are here to stay.
Then, I realized, with AI, I can simply teach it my notions of music theory and mathematically explore all permutations of tasteful chord transitions based on rules of voice leading, harmony, melody, and counterpoint. It can then derive new chord progressions. It can write code to create synths and drums. It can write music from scratch in my new tonal system, the golden tonal system. With AI, I am empowered to forge paths into a new realm of microtonal music that tastefully extends upon standard Western harmony with new colors. I could have sat down and figured it out in under 40 hours at any point over the past decade, but I never did. And now it takes a few minutes of human time with a few hours of computation time.
It's at this point worth noting the history of how I discovered this scale. It all started from someone claiming that bliss occurs when our brain is in a state of harmony, where the brain waves were in golden ratio of each other. When I asked a neuroscientist about this, he angrily said it was the worst idea, since harmonics (with rational numbers) lead to states of epilepsy. However, the irrational numbers in concert produce the most unstable and wobbling overtones. They are mathematically defined as the least stable chords. Which means, they are not bad, they are meant to be passing chords. However, in live, we are always passing through different states of mind. Perhaps it helps to have chaotic activation energy of unstable overtones in our brain? If binaural beats can induce frequencies into our brain, then perhaps a new musical system can induce states of bliss (or inspire others to figure out the optimal way to do this). And even if all of this is wrong, it coincidentally led to a new form of art based on mathematical principles.
Yes, it is dissonant, but considering their irrational foundation, it is remarkable that they sound as good as they do, in my opinion. This is foreign to our ears. In the early 1000's, Gregorian chant would have found the major chord to be distasteful, as they often sang in perfect fifths. The human ear has evolved to listen to more complex structures over time. Today, we even hear polyrhythms in pop music from time to time. In the future, our ears may evolve to listen to more sophisticated music with a wider emotional range of expression.
